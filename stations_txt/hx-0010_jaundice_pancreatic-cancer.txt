=== EXAMINER INFORMATION ===
Questions
1. What is the most likely diagnosis and what differential diagnoses would you consider in this patient?
Answer: Most likely diagnosis is pancreatic cancer (head of pancreas adenocarcinoma causing obstructive jaundice). Differential diagnoses include cholangiocarcinoma / ampullary tumour, choledocholithiasis (gallstone obstruction), benign biliary stricture, or drug-induced/viral hepatitis.
2. What initial investigations would you arrange to evaluate this patient?
Answer: Urgent bloods (FBC, U&Es, LFTs showing obstructive/cholestatic picture, coagulation screen, CA 19-9) and urgent imaging such as an abdominal ultrasound scan or urgent triple-phase / pancreatic-protocol contrast-enhanced CT of the abdomen.
3. If imaging confirms a mass in the head of the pancreas, how should this patient be managed?
Answer: Urgent referral via the suspected cancer pathway (two-week wait) to the hepatobiliary multidisciplinary team (MDT); consideration of biliary decompression (e.g. ERCP with biliary stenting) for severe jaundice/pruritus; staging investigations; and assessment for surgical resection (e.g. Whipple procedure) versus palliative oncology care.
